   This is the most recent version of this level modification for Jill of the Jungle. If you have any of
the previous versions, good for you, but this one has addressed some of the issues I found with the earlier
releases (mostly sub-par level design, and in one instance a bonus section was impossible to complete).
I've also adjusted parts of the hub-level after some feedback from those who have played previous versions.

   This game should work on any system capable of running an IBM-compatible DOS environment. To use this
game on a modern system, you will need to run it through an emulator such as DosBox. There is more info
about this later in this document.

---Original text follows:

   Hello! If you're reading this, I hope you've managed to survive the worst that 2020 threw at you.
This level pack was started during my time in COVID-19 lockdown, when I finally decided to try my hand
at something I'd wanted to do for DECADES but never really had the means to do until now. It's surprising
what a global pandemic can do to your motivation, hey? Seriously, though, if there was one thing that I
learned from being stuck at home, and only being able to leave for essential tasks, while millions of
people were suffering and struggling in ways I could only imagine... it was to never take anything for
granted ever again. I truly hope that everyone who reads this has managed to keep themselves and all of
their loved ones safe, healthy and sane.

   I played the original Jill Of The Jungle shareware episode on a public computer in my school library,
all the way back in 1992... and was captivated by how varied and thoughtful all of the puzzles were. Of course
nowadays the game seems so primitive and "blocky", but I realised that the game had a lot of potential; I
pored over the level structure, trying to find new secrets and strategies, and pondered at the preview levels
wondering what the sequel games would be like (I didn't have my own computer, nor the money or the means to
buy the sequels until much, much later).

   To think that this simple game was one that put Epic on the map! I wonder how the original team feels about
this game now - do they still remember how it felt to put their art and code together, to create stories and
settings for action and puzzles, and to have their hard work pay off with years of support and happy memories
from the fans? Or do they just look back at Jill as a means to an end, a mere money-earner to keep the
momentum going?

   Many players have gone on to study and analyze the workings of Jill, along with many other DOS games of the era.
Someone named Rory built the earliest level editor, with a promise to make a more sophisticated version once
the paid orders came in (no such editor came about, it seems). Another tried to recreate Jill using an updated
engine they'd built themselves - and if I could find that video on YouTube, I'd really like to know how that
project worked out! But while many other games of the same time period wound up having editors made for them, Jill
remained an unusual title - one that was reasonably popular, but relatively untouched by fans keen on making
their own adventures.

   It wasn't until someone named Malvineous, a member of the RGB Classic Games forum, dicovered that the original
game had a built-in editor that modding the game became more realistic. It was still difficult, but at least a
more solid framework was there, and led to people gaining a better understanding of how the game worked.
More recently, K1n9 Duk3 created a more user-friendly level editor that allowed creators to edit tiles and objects,
as well as implement the functions that made the levels work as intended. 

   Meanwhile, I'd never really joined in on the discussion directly, but I'd been watching a lot of the interest
and research unfold over the years. I found myself coming up with interesting ideas and new approaches to the older
game style. And when the world seemed to close in on itself, I thought, why not try to put all the pieces together
and contribute something for those who might appreciate it?

   I wanted to create something that would compare and pay tribute to the original games... something that
would take the examples given by the original series, and build upon them in a way that seemed logical and appealing.
It has gone through a few iterations, and it's not finished yet - but there's enough here to provide a proof of
concept, and should be engaging enough for you to get the general idea.

   TL;DR: some tired old Gen X got bored during 2020 and tried to make a level mod of a 29-year-old DOS game WOOT

---How to actually play the game

   This game is a modifcation of a piece of DOS software, so it should run fine on any system that can support
MS-DOS. Therefore, it should run on any hardware that could run the original Jill of the Jungle game.

   ...To get JillEPIC running on modern hardware, you'll need an additional program called an "emulator" to let
the game think it's installed on an original PC. There are many available, depending on your system - for
instance, a free program called DosBox is available for computers running a Windows environment, and there are
many variants of the program that can offer different options for your needs. Simply start the emulator program,
and load up the "jillEPIC.exe" file (check the instructions that came with the emulator if you are unsure how to
do this). I've also included a "jillepic.ico" icon for the game if you wish to use the custom icon on your
desktop.

   The game launcher will offer you some graphics and audio settings upon starting the game; it's up to you how
you have them set up, but I recommend using the SoundBlaster sounds and music and VGA graphics. Obviously.

   As for the actual gameplay, the premise is simple: guide Jill through the levels, collecting items and power-ups
to help you on your quest. Keyboard commands are provided in-game as you need them, and if you need further help, 
pressing the f1 key will bring up a detailed instruction menu.


---Further Hints

 * The Enchanted Palace is a mysterious, maze-like structure; inside its convoluted halls are several magical portals,
   each of which lead to different environments, hazards and puzzles. Note that, unlike previous Jill titles, it is
   NOT necessary to play *every* level available to you - in fact, you can play the levels in any order you like, so
   long as you can find them first!

 * The main objective of this game is to try to collect as many Gems (pink sparkling crystals) as you can in each
   level. That's not unusual for a Jill game - but in this case, the *amount* of gems you can find varies on how
   well you play the game!

 * Most Gems are easy to find in the levels, and you should be able to make good progress with standard play. However,
   careful exploration and thought can provide you with more chances to find extra Gems hidden in the level. Some Gems
   are hidden in out of the way places, and some routes offer better chances of keeping the Gems you have!

 * Gems are the ONLY objects you can take with you when leaving a level and returning to the map, so the more skilled
   and careful you are, the more gems you will have for the Palace, and the more progress you will make!

 * At first, the portals that lead to new levels will be wide open - but as you progress through the Palace,
   new sections and portals will be locked off by Gem Gates, and the Gems in later levels will be harder to find
   and keep. Some sections of the Palace are locked off by multiple gates, and sign posts will tell you how many
   Gems are needed to open these sections.

 * Your progress through the Palace will depend on your skill on finding Gems; so too, will your final outcome. If you
   perform poorly, or give up too easily, you will get a lesser conclusion to your quest; do well, and you will get
   a better outcome - but only if you do well!

 * Each level revolves around a different theme; some are just standard platform game environments, while others are
   more abstract. Some levels are short, others are long; some are simple on the surface, but yield further treasures
   when you search off the beaten path; and sometimes, you'll be able to go back over familiar ground and see
   things in a whole new perspective!

 * The maximum number of items you can have in your Inventory at any time is 15. Any items you get after that are
   not counted and are discarded. This is VERY important to consider when on the Map - if you already have a lot of
   Gems in your Inventory, consider using them to open some Gem Gates before entering a new level.

 * Don't forget, you can save your game at any time!

 * Even when you've finished the game, there may still be puzzles, secrets and challenges you may have overlooked.
   Why not go back in again and see what else you can find?
